# CryoSat Texture Style Guide

CryoSat should look like it shipped in the same box as Mekanism. Base everything on Mekanism's steel
and add our own accents so a player can tell a CryoSat machine apart at a glance, without ever
mistaking it for a Mekanism tier.

This guide is written for whoever generates textures, human or agent. Read it fully once, then keep
`tools/textures/palette.py` and `tools/textures/texlib.py` open while working.

## Tooling

| File | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `tools/textures/palette.py` | Every color you are allowed to use, as ramps from dark to light |
| `tools/textures/texlib.py` | Drawing primitives (`Tex` class): shells, plates, windows, vents, frost, foil, tier pips |
| `tools/textures/preview.py` | Upscaled preview sheets, optionally next to Mekanism references and as 3x3 tilings |
| `tools/textures/model_preview.py` | Isometric render (SE and NW views) of a Blockbench JSON model with its textures |
| `tools/textures/fetch_mekanism_refs.sh` | Sparse clone of Mekanism's textures and models into `.mekanism-refs/` (gitignored) |
| `tools/textures/examples/` | Reference generator scripts. Copy their structure |

Textures are generated by Python scripts, one script per block or item family, living in
`tools/textures/gen/`. A script must be deterministic (seed the `Tex` with the texture name) so
reruns do not churn the PNGs in git. Run scripts from the repo root with `python3`.

### Workflow for every texture

1. Fetch references once: `tools/textures/fetch_mekanism_refs.sh`.
2. Find the closest Mekanism equivalent (see "Reference map" below) and look at it upscaled.
3. Write or extend a generator in `tools/textures/gen/`, built from `texlib` primitives.
4. Render a preview with references on top and `--tile`, then actually look at the image:
   `python3 tools/textures/preview.py build/preview.png --tile --ref <mekanism pngs> -- <our pngs>`
5. Check against the rules below. Iterate until it passes. Then commit the script and the PNGs.

## The Mekanism look, measured

These numbers come from analysing Mekanism 10.7's own PNGs.

* **Resolution.** Block faces and items are 16x16. Model texture sheets for 3D machines are 32x32
  (larger machines use 64x64) and are UV mapped in Blockbench.
* **Neutral steel.** Machine bodies are pure greys (R = G = B), never tinted. Values cluster into
  bands: void `#0d` to `#18`, frame `#24` to `#35`, panel `#40` to `#56`, plate `#5d` to `#73`,
  glint `#7f` and up. Casings for multiblocks run lighter (`#5b` to `#9d`, faintly warm).
* **Noise, not flats.** Every surface is a dithered mix of 3 to 5 neighbouring values within one
  band, with per pixel jitter of about plus or minus 2 to 4. A flat fill instantly looks wrong. Use
  `Tex.fill`, which does this for you.
* **Light from the top left.** Raised parts are lighter on the top and left edges and darker on the
  bottom and right. Recessed parts are the opposite. Keep the contrast gentle: a bevel highlight is
  one band step above its surface, not a white line.
* **Composition of a 1x1 machine:**
  * *Front:* a dark frame, a raised plate holding a black window (with 3 to 5 diagonal glare specks),
    and a 2 pixel checkered strip near the bottom.
  * *Sides:* a lighter plate covering the upper half to two thirds, sometimes with a diagonal cut,
    over a darker lower section with seams or vents.
  * *Top:* a plate with a recessed slot or vent.
  * *Bottom:* a plain inset square.
  * *Active state:* only the window changes (a glowing interior), animated with `.mcmeta`.
* **Accents are small.** Color shows up as an inlay, a valve, a stripe or an LED, at roughly 5 to 15
  percent of a face. Tier blocks (induction cells, energy cubes) are the exception, where the tier
  color floods the face.
* **Hazard stripe.** Factories and tanks use a yellow (`#caa84e`) and black band, 2 pixels tall.
* **Emissive overlays.** Things that glow (LEDs, screens) live in a separate `_led` or `_glow`
  texture that the model renders fullbright. Keep the base texture readable without them.

## CryoSat accents

Mekanism's tier hues are taken, and our accents must never be confused with them:

| Tier | Valve ramp | Hue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic | `#266343` to `#1ca760` | mint green |
| Advanced | `#662525` to `#9f2f2b` | red |
| Elite | `#2d4859` to `#3d98a2` | teal (about 187 degrees) |
| Ultimate | `#3d2748` to `#733b7b` | violet (about 290 degrees) |

### Cryogenics: Cryo Cobalt and frost

* **Cryo Cobalt** (`COBALT`, `#16204f` to `#7391ee`) is the signature accent for anything cold. It
  sits at about 228 degrees, well apart from elite teal and ultimate violet. Keep it saturated:
  greyed cobalt drifts toward elite. Use it for trims (`Tex.cobalt_trim`, at least 2 pixels tall),
  pipe collars and valve rings.
* **Coolant glow** (`CRYO_GLOW`) is the color of visible cryogenic liquid in windows and gauges
  (`Tex.coolant_window`). The active state of a cryo machine swaps the black window for coolant glow.
* **Frost** (`FROST`) is crusted rime. It always sits on top of steel, never as a base color. Use a
  connected crust along the top edge with short drips (`Tex.frost`) or a corner patch
  (`Tex.frost_corner`). The colder the machine, the more frost, but never more than about a third of
  a face. Never sprinkle isolated white pixels across a surface: that reads as snow or noise.

### Space hardware: MLI gold, solar cells, ordnance red

Space hardware still uses Mekanism steel as its base. On top of it:

* **MLI Gold** (`GOLD`, `#4a3410` to `#fff0b8`) is crinkled multilayer insulation foil, the
  signature of anything that goes to orbit. Draw it with `Tex.gold_foil`, which makes small faceted
  cells with dark creases. Apply it as wrapped panels inside a steel frame, not as a full face.
  It is amber leaning on purpose, so it does not merge with the yellow hazard stripe.
* **Solar** (`SOLAR`) is the blue black of solar cells, with a grid of `SOLAR[0]` lines every 4
  pixels and a sparse specular pixel per cell.
* **Ordnance** (`ORDNANCE`) is a dark warning red for weapon satellites and kinetic rods only.
  It is deliberately darker than the advanced tier red. Pair it with hazard stripes.

### Tiers on CryoSat machines

When a CryoSat block has tiers, show the tier with Mekanism's own `TIER` ramps as a small inlay
(`Tex.tier_pip`, about a 4x4 to 6x6 area), a valve ring, or a stripe on a 3D model. The rest of the
block stays steel plus our accent. Glowing tier LEDs use `TIER_GLOW`.

## 3D models (space machinery)

Launch pads, rockets, satellites and the mission control desk use Blockbench style Java block models,
following Mekanism's conventions (see `.mekanism-refs/src/main/resources/assets/mekanism/models/block/`,
for example `chemical_washer.json` and `digital_miner.json`):

* One 32x32 (or 64x64 for big models) texture sheet per model, referenced as `"all"`, with UVs in
  the 0 to 16 space. Shared bits can reference extra sheets (`ports`, `_elements`).
* Name every element and organise them in `groups`, so the file opens cleanly in Blockbench.
* `"render_type": "minecraft:cutout"`, plus a `particle` texture (use a steel casing texture).
* Put emissive parts (screens, LEDs) on a separate `_led` sheet.
* Elements must stay within -16 to 32 on every axis (a 3x3x3 block volume around the origin).
  Anything bigger is either split across several blocks of a multiblock or rendered by a block
  entity renderer. Decide this with whoever writes the Java side before modelling.
* Tier accents on models go on a small separate texture (like Mekanism's
  `chemical_tank_<tier>_valve.png`) so one model serves every tier by swapping one texture.
* These models are seen briefly before launch, so favour clean silhouettes over detail: 10 to 40
  elements is plenty.
* Always render the model with `tools/textures/model_preview.py MODEL.json OUT.png` and look at it,
  next to a render of a comparable Mekanism model (the digital miner and chemical washer are good
  benchmarks). Use `--tex valve=<png>` to check each tier's accent texture.

## Checklist before committing a texture

* [ ] 16x16 (or the model sheet size), RGBA PNG, and animated strips have a `.mcmeta`.
* [ ] Only palette colors (plus `texlib` jitter). No pure white, no pure black.
* [ ] Steel is neutral grey and noisy. No flat fills larger than about 3 pixels.
* [ ] Light comes from the top left, and bevels are gentle.
* [ ] The accent covers at most about 15 percent of the face, and no tier hue is used outside tier marking.
* [ ] It sits naturally next to the Mekanism reference in the preview sheet.
* [ ] The 3x3 tiling shows no ugly seams (for blocks that tile, such as casings).
* [ ] The generator script is committed next to the PNG and reproduces it exactly.
